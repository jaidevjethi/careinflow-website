## A town where the working day decides everything

Unjha is a trading town, and the market sets the clock. People are occupied through the middle of the day and free at the two ends of it.

That single fact governs more about a clinic's online presence here than any amount of design does, because it determines when a patient can actually come to you — and therefore whether your published hours are helping or quietly turning people away.

## The hours field is the whole game

If your listing says nine to six and you genuinely see patients at seven in the morning and again at eight in the evening, Google is telling every searcher that you are closed at precisely the moments they are free.

Nobody reports this. They simply go elsewhere, and you never learn it happened.

So we set the real hours, including the seasonal shifts around the trading calendar and the festival closures everyone forgets to update, and then we keep them current every month. It is the least interesting work we do and it moves more appointments in this town than anything else.

## Word of mouth still ends in a search

Most practices in Unjha are built on reputation, and that reputation genuinely works. But follow it through to what actually happens.

Someone recommends you. The patient types your name into their phone to get the number. What loads at that moment either confirms the recommendation or undermines it: a complete listing with real photographs of a practice that plainly exists and is plainly open, or a bare entry with no photographs, hours nobody has touched in two years and an unanswered review from last spring.

A website does not replace word of mouth here. It stops it leaking on the last step.

## The patients with no local knowledge

The temple brings visitors from across Gujarat and beyond throughout the year, and some of them need a clinic while they are in town.

They know nobody. They cannot ask a neighbour. They take the top result that looks current and open, and they take it in seconds.

That is a winnable position in Unjha, because very few practices here have real photographs, correct hours and answered reviews all at once. The three of them together are usually enough.

## What the groundwork looks like

A page for each treatment you offer, written in the words patients type rather than the words on your qualification. Hours that match the market day. A listing claimed, completed and maintained. Reviews answered, drafted for your approval and never posted blind. Details identical everywhere they appear, so Google stops hedging about which version of you is real.

Business families judge a counterparty quickly and they judge consistency. The same instinct applies to your practice online.
