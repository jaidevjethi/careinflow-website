## This is a sample. The clinic in it does not exist.

Gati Physiotherapy & Rehabilitation is not a practice. There is no clinic on SG Highway, the phone number does not ring, and Dr. Riya Mehta is not a person. We built the whole thing so that a physiotherapist deciding who to hire could open a finished site instead of judging a mockup.

Some things on it are invented in a way worth naming, because a real clinic cannot copy them:

- **The "90% surgical avoidance" figure on the homepage.** It is a placeholder. A real practice publishes a number like that only if it can show the audit behind it, or it does not publish it at all.
- **The three patient recovery stories.** Written for the sample. Real ones come from real patients, with consent, and they are worth ten times as much for exactly that reason.
- **The insurer and TPA logos.** Placeholders for the panels a clinic is genuinely empanelled with.

We would not put any of those on a live site without the evidence, and we do not invent them for clients. What this sample is showing you is the design, the structure and the build. The judgement about which claims a practice can honestly make is part of the work, and it belongs to the practice.

## Why physiotherapy is the hardest specialty to organise

Most clinics can be described by what they do. A dental practice does root canals; a dermatology clinic does laser resurfacing. Name the treatment and you have named the page.

Physiotherapy breaks that, because the treatment and the complaint have almost no words in common. The clinic performs mobilisation and progressive loading. The patient has a shoulder that will not lift. Build the website around the first vocabulary and it will be accurate, professional and completely undiscoverable.

So this site is built around the second. The primary navigation asks "Where does it hurt?" and offers four parts of the body. Every condition is titled the way a patient would say it out loud. The clinical terms are all still there, one bracket later, doing the job of proving the clinic knows what it is talking about.

We [argue this on our physiotherapy page](/specialties/physiotherapy-clinics/) and have done for a while. This is the first place you can see it working.

The build is static, and no JavaScript framework is shipped to the browser at all. The only script on the site runs the mobile menu, and it handles the escape key and a click outside it properly. The FAQ that opens and closes, and every link in the condition mesh, is plain HTML doing what plain HTML has always done. That is why it opens quickly on a bad connection, and it is how we build for clinics.

## What we would change for a real clinic

This is a sample, and being straight about its gaps is more useful than pretending it has none.

- **It publishes no prices.** That is the biggest gap. A patient comparing two physiotherapy clinics is trying to work out a total, not a per-session rate, and a clinic that publishes a session figure alongside a typical course length answers a question its competitors are ducking. A real build would carry both.
- **The condition pages are short.** The structure is right and it transfers unchanged, but a hundred words per condition is where a real practice's own experience goes: what they see most, what they tell patients in the room, how long it actually takes here.
- **The photography is stock and the practitioner is invented.** Both get replaced. Stock imagery is a placeholder rather than a choice, and a patient can usually tell the difference between a real treatment room and a bought one.
- **It is English only.** Our dental and dermatology work runs in English and Gujarati throughout. A physiotherapy practice in Gujarat needs the same, and the recovery instructions especially.

## What this sample is for

A physiotherapist, an orthopaedic surgeon running a rehab unit, or a sports clinic can open this on their own phone and see the standard before spending anything. Nothing here is a drawing. Load the conditions page, pick the body part that matches your own commonest complaint, and see whether the page you land on is the page you would want a patient to read.
