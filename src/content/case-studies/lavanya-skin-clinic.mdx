## This is a sample. Nothing written in it is true.

There is no Lavanya Skin Studio. There is no Dr. Pooja Patel, and nobody
connected to this site is a gold medalist from B.J. Medical College. The
address is a real building with an invented clinic inside it. The four
figures on the homepage under the heading "audit metric" — the session
count, the satisfaction percentage, the years without an incident — are
numbers we made up to fill four boxes.

We are saying this first, and plainly, because the rest of our site argues
that a practice should never publish a claim it cannot support, and it
would be worth very little if we quietly made an exception for our own
portfolio.

## So what is this sample for?

The design and the build. Open it on your phone and judge the things that
survive having invented words in them: how fast it opens, how the layout
holds at the size you are actually reading, whether the booking button is
ever more than a thumb away, how a treatment page is structured, how the
language switcher behaves.

Those are the parts we are showing you, and they are the parts that
transfer to your practice unchanged. A real build starts from your
treatments, your city and your patients, and the words are written with
you in [week two](/process) before any of this visual work begins.

## What would we change for a real clinic?

If a dermatology practice hired us tomorrow, the copy on this sample would
not survive the first review, and it is worth being specific about why.

**The regulatory claims would go.** "US-FDA approved" appears across the
sample, and the US Food and Drug Administration does not certify clinics
in Gujarat. "Ahmedabad's premier skin specialist" is a superiority claim
of exactly the kind that India's medical advertising rules exist to
restrict. Neither belongs on a page with a real doctor's name on it.

**The invented numbers would go.** A number presented as an audited
measurement has to be an audited measurement. If a practice has genuinely
completed twelve thousand sessions, we will publish that and say where the
figure came from. If it has not, the page is stronger without a number at
all.

**The language would come down several floors.** The sample says
"pro-growth cytokinetic stimulation" where it means PRP might help thinning
hair, and "collagen architectural matrix modeling" where it means the skin
repairs itself afterwards. That reads as expertise to the person who wrote
it and as noise to a patient deciding whether to book. Our
[writing](/services/healthcare-websites) is plain by policy: symptom first,
cost second, procedure name last, because that is the order a worried
person thinks in.

Which is the honest summary of this whole page. The sample shows what we
can build. What we would actually write for you is on the rest of this
site.

## Why publish a sample at all?

Because a practice owner comparing three studios can open this on their own
phone, at their own network speed, at eleven at night, and form a first-hand
opinion in under a minute. That is worth more than any description we could
write, and more than a slide of flattering screenshots.

The real work is on the [Pramukh Dental case study](/work/pramukh-dental),
where the clinic, the treatments and the results are all genuine. Read that
one for what we do. Open this one for what it looks like when it is
finished.
