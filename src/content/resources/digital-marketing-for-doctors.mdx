Most clinics are sold digital marketing from whichever end is easiest to
sell: a package of daily posts, or a budget for ads. A patient meets your
practice from several directions at once. They search, see your Google
listing, open your website, and often come across your videos along the
way. Each of those does a different job, and a clinic that is missing one
feels it whatever it spends on the others.

This guide sets out what each piece does, a sensible order if you are
starting from nothing, what each costs, what to leave until later, and how
to tell whether any of it is working. It applies to an individual doctor's
practice as much as to a multispecialty clinic.

## Where should a clinic start?

If you are starting from nothing, this is the order that follows how
patients check a practice. If yours already has one of these in good
shape, skip to the next.

### 1. Your Google Business Profile

This is where most patients first see you: the panel with your name, map,
photographs, timings and reviews. It decides whether you make a patient's
shortlist before your website is ever opened.

It is free, and you can set it up yourself. The essentials are the right
primary category, every service listed, accurate hours including festival
days, real photographs, and replies to reviews. Our
[guide to Google Business Profile](/resources/google-business-profile-for-clinics/)
walks through each one. If you would rather hand it over, we rebuild a

loads quickly on a phone, and puts WhatsApp and a call button within reach.

Reels and posts let patients hear you before they meet you, which builds a

The order is a default, not a rule. A doctor who already records videos
can start with content; a clinic with a good website and a neglected
listing should start with the listing. Start with what your clinic is
missing, not with what someone is selling.

## What should a clinic skip at first?

- **Paid ads before the basics.** An advert sends people to your listing
  and website. If those do not answer a patient's questions, the money
  leaks out the other side. Get the destination right first.
- **Buying followers or reviews.** Both are visible to anyone who looks,
  and fake reviews break Google's rules. Our guide to
  [online reputation for doctors](/resources/online-reputation-for-doctors/)
  covers the honest way to collect reviews.
- **A template that lists every specialty on one page.** A patient with a
  specific problem wants a page about that problem.
- **A posting schedule you cannot keep.** Two good reels a week, kept up,
  beats daily posts that stop in the second month.

## How do you know if it is working?

Measure what reaches the practice, not what looks good in a report:

- **Calls, direction requests and website visits from your Google
  listing.** Your Business Profile shows these each month, for free.
- **WhatsApp messages and calls from your website**, and which treatment
  page they came from.
- **What patients tell reception.** "I found you on Google" and "I saw your
  video" are the most honest analytics a clinic has. Ask, and write it
  down.

Expect the pieces to move at different speeds. Listing corrections show
within days, a website works from the day it launches, local search moves
over months, and content builds slowly. Be wary of anyone who promises a
ranking or a number of new patients: Google says no one can pay for or
request a better local position, and we do not guarantee one.
