If you measured where patients first see your practice, the winner would
almost certainly not be your website. It would be your Google Business
Profile: the panel with the map, stars and photos that appears when
someone searches your name or "clinic near me".

Most practices have never deliberately managed it. This guide covers what
matters, in order.

## What is a Google Business Profile?

Google Business Profile (GBP, formerly Google My Business) is the free
listing Google shows in Maps and local search results. You do not create
the demand, patients are already searching, you only control what they
find. An unclaimed or unmanaged profile still appears; it is just wrong
more often.

## How do you get your clinic to show on Google Search?

Start by searching your clinic's name, because the answer changes what you
do next.

**If a listing already appears, claim it. Do not create a new one.** Google
often generates listings for practices from other sources long before the
practice notices, and creating a second one gives you two competing
listings for the same clinic. That splits your reviews, confuses the map,
and is genuinely tedious to unpick later. On the existing listing, look for
"Claim this business" or "Own this business?".

**If nothing appears at all**, create the profile from Google's own Business
Profile page. You will need the practice name exactly as it appears on your
signage, the street address, a category, and a phone number that reaches
the clinic.

Either way it ends in verification, which is Google confirming you are
really there. For clinics this is usually a short video showing the
premises, your signage and something proving you can access the space.
Nothing is public until verification completes, so it is worth doing the
day you start rather than a fortnight later.

One decision specific to healthcare: a practice and an individual
practitioner can each hold their own listing. A single-doctor clinic
usually wants one. A practice with several named consultants patients
search for by name may reasonably want a listing for the practice and one
per practitioner, kept consistent with each other.

## Why does your category matter most?

Your primary category tells Google what searches you belong in. "Dental
clinic" versus "Dentist" versus "Cosmetic dentist" produce different
visibility. The most common mistake we see is a generic category
("Medical clinic") on a specialist practice, which forfeits specialist
searches entirely. Set the most specific accurate primary category, then
add secondary categories for genuine additional services.

## What photos should a clinic upload?

Listings with real, recent photographs earn more calls and direction
requests. Patients are looking for evidence: the actual reception, the
actual equipment, the actual people. Upload real photos, replace them as
the practice changes, and never use stock imagery, patients recognize it
instantly, and it reads as having something to hide.

## Why do wrong opening hours cost you visits?

"Open now" filters results. Wrong hours do worse than lose a visit, a
patient who arrives at a closed clinic remembers it. Holiday hours (Diwali,
Uttarayan, public holidays) need updating every time, which is exactly the
kind of small recurring task that a [managed profile](/services/google-business-profile)
handles on schedule.

## How should a clinic handle reviews?

Reviews decide the first cut. Three practical rules:

1. **Make reviewing effortless** for genuinely happy patients: a QR code
   at the desk or a WhatsApp link after a visit.
2. **Reply to everything**: including, especially, critical reviews.
   A calm, professional reply is read by every future patient.
3. **Never buy reviews.** Google detects spikes and purchased patterns;
   removal and lost trust cost far more than the shortcut earned. Patient
   privacy also limits what a reply may reveal, replies should never
   confirm details of anyone's treatment.

## Why must your details match your website?

Google cross-checks your listing against your website and other
directories. Name, address and phone number must match exactly,
everywhere. Mismatches, an old number on a directory, a differently
spelled address, quietly erode local ranking. This is one reason profile
management and [local SEO](/services/local-seo) work best as one effort.

## What ten-minute check can you do today?

Search your clinic's name in an incognito browser tab and check: Is the
category specific and correct? Are the photos real and less than a year
old? Are today's hours right? Is the newest review answered? Does the
website link work, and does the site show the same phone number?

If any answer is no, you have found this month's highest-return marketing
task.
