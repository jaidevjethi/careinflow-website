Your next patient has probably already seen your practice — on a phone
screen, at night, next to two competitors. What they saw decided whether
they called you or someone else.

This is not a marketing claim; it is simply how people behave now. Before
trusting a stranger with their teeth, skin or eyes, they check. Here is
what that checking actually looks like.

## The three surfaces a patient meets

A patient rarely types your clinic's name first. The journey usually runs
through three surfaces:

**The Google listing.** A search like "dentist near me" shows a map and
three results. Rating, photo, distance, "open now" — the first cut happens
here, in seconds, before your website is ever seen.

**The website.** Patients who survive the first cut tap through. Now they
are looking for substance: does this clinic treat what I have? What does
it cost? Who is the doctor? Does this feel current and professional?

**The AI answer.** Increasingly, patients ask an assistant — "best skin
clinic in Ahmedabad?" — and read a synthesized answer. Those answers are
assembled from the structured facts clinics publish. Most publish none.

## What patients actually check

Watch someone choose a clinic and the checklist is remarkably consistent:

1. **Rating and review count** — not just the stars, but whether anyone
   from the clinic replies, and how they handle criticism.
2. **Photos** — real photos of the actual clinic beat stock images of
   smiling models, every time. Patients can tell.
3. **Hours** — "open now" is a filter. Wrong holiday hours cost real
   visits.
4. **The treatment they need** — a patient with a specific problem looks
   for a page about that problem. A generic "our services" list reads as
   "we have nothing specific to say".
5. **Cost signals** — even a price range or "what affects the cost"
   explanation builds more trust than silence.

## What quietly loses them

The failures are rarely dramatic. A site that takes six seconds to load on
mobile data. A last-updated-in-2021 photo gallery. An unanswered one-star
review sitting at the top. A number that rings at reception nobody staffs
at lunch. Each one is small; together they send the patient one listing
down.

## What to do about it

Start with an honest audit of the three surfaces. Search your own clinic
the way a patient would — on a phone, on mobile data, logged out. Check
your [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile) for
accuracy, load your own website and count the seconds, and ask an AI
assistant about clinics in your area to see whether you exist there at
all.
