## Who is this for?

Doctors who already explain things well, in the chair or on camera, and do not
have the time or the tools to turn that into something worth posting. If your
phone holds videos that never went out, or went out unedited, this is for you.

It is healthcare content only. We make reels and posts for clinics and for
nobody else, so the edit understands what a patient needs to hear, and what a
doctor should not say.

## What do you send us?

Less than you might think:

- The footage, recorded on your phone. One question per video works best.
- The topic, and the patient question it answers
- The language you want the captions in
- Your logo and contact details, once
- What you want patients to do next: call, send a WhatsApp message, or book

For a post or a carousel, a few lines on the topic, or a photo of a clinic
update, is enough. You check every piece for medical accuracy before it goes
anywhere.

## What do you get back?

Publish-ready, patient-facing content:

- **Reels.** Patient explainers, treatment explainers, and patient testimonials where your council's rules allow them and the patient has agreed in writing.
- **Educational posts.** One useful thing per post.
- **Carousels.** One topic, across up to six slides.
- **Clinic creatives.** A package flyer, a health camp, a change of hours.
- **Captions and covers.** Written and designed for every reel.

## Gujarati or English captions?

Your choice, reel by reel, and it should follow your patients rather than your
own preference:

- **Gujarati** when you speak Gujarati on camera and your patients mostly read it. It is how they hear you in the chair.
- **English** when your patients are younger, more urban or mixed, or when the treatment names are English words anyway.
- **Both**, sometimes: Gujarati captions with the key medical term kept in English, so a patient can search for it later.

We do not claim one language reaches more people than the other. Tell us who
your patients are, we will suggest one, and you decide.

## One recording, a month of content

Most doctors think of one video as one post. One good explanation is usually a
great deal more than that.

A dentist who records eight to ten minutes on gum disease has given us a main
reel, a shorter reel, a carousel of warning signs and an educational post. The
same knowledge can then become a story, an answer to a question the front desk
hears every week, a section of the treatment page on the website, and an update

Not every recording yields all of that, and a package counts reels, posts and

## How does content help your website and Google listing?

A patient deciding between clinics checks more than one place: your Google
listing, your website, and often your Instagram. The same knowledge serves all
three. An explanation you recorded for a reel can become the answer on your
treatment page, and a short update on your Google Business Profile.

That does not make every post or page a search result. Where a clinic appears
depends on relevance, distance, prominence, competition and reviews, and on the
website and the listing being right. Content gives those places something
useful to say. If your [website](/services/healthcare-websites/) or your
[Google listing](/services/google-business-profile/) is the weaker link, the
[free digital review](/contact/) will say so, and so will we.

## How are patient testimonials handled?

Patient experience videos are a format we edit, and they come with more rules
than any other, so the first question is whether one can be published at all.
The National Medical Commission's guidelines on advertising, issued on
6 October 2026, do not allow registered medical practitioners to request or
share patient testimonials to promote their practice on social media, and they
say a patient's consent alone does not make one permissible. Dentists and
physiotherapists answer to their own councils, with their own rules. Before we
edit a testimonial we check with you which rules apply to your practice, and if
they rule it out, we say so rather than edit it.

Where one is allowed:

- The patient agrees in writing to appear, and to the video being shared.
- Nothing identifies them beyond what they agreed to: no names, phone numbers, reports or appointment details on screen.
- We do not add words, claims or results the patient did not say.
- Nothing suggests a treatment works for everyone, and there are no staged before-and-after outcomes.
- You approve the final edit before it is published.

The rules on advertising apply to you, and it is your registration at risk,
not ours. If something in a clip reads like a promise, we flag it before it
goes out.

## How is patient privacy protected?

Healthcare content has limits other industries do not, and we hold to them even
when it costs reach:

- No clinical photographs without clear written consent
- No patient stories that could identify someone who has not agreed
- No claims about outcomes that cannot be supported

A post that embarrasses a patient is worse for your practice than no post at
all.
