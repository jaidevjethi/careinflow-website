## What does social media actually do for a practice?

It confirms. A patient who found you through search checks your profile
before calling. If the last post is two years old, that hesitates them. If
it shows a practice that explains things clearly and posts steadily, it
settles them. That is a real job, and it is worth doing properly.

What it is not is a substitute for a website or a Google listing, and we
will still say so if those are the weaker link. But we no longer refuse the
work on that basis: plenty of practices have a perfectly good site and an
empty Instagram, and the empty Instagram is the thing costing them.

## What does the social media plan include?

Every month: a content strategy and a calendar built from your specialty,
your treatments and what your patients keep asking. Instagram profile
optimisation: bio, positioning, call to action, contact details and
highlight structure. Up to four content pieces a week, around sixteen a
month. Three reel edits from footage you provide, with captions, text
overlays and hooks. Caption writing, topic research, scheduling and
publishing. Then a review of what performed, and what next month should do
differently.

### The mix follows the strategy

The monthly content mix is decided by the strategy. It may include static
posts, carousels, educational graphics, treatment content and other
suitable formats. **The package is a content volume and a strategy, not a
fixed number of any one format.** A seven-slide researched carousel and a
static post are not the same production work, and pricing them as though
they were is how a content plan quietly stops being any good.

## What do we post for a healthcare practice?

The best clinic content is the answer to a question you already give ten
times a week in the chair:

- What a treatment involves, step by step
- What recovery actually feels like, honestly
- When a symptom means "come in today"
- What affects the cost of a procedure
- Practical clinic updates, new equipment, festival hours, a new doctor

We write these as short, plain-language posts. You approve everything
before it goes out, and you correct anything clinically inaccurate.

## How is patient privacy protected in content?

Healthcare social media has limits that other industries do not. We hold
to them even when it costs reach:

- No clinical photographs without clear written consent
- No patient stories that could identify someone who has not agreed
- No replies that confirm whether a person was treated at your clinic
- No claims about outcomes we cannot support

A post that embarrasses a patient is worse for your practice than no post
at all.

## What does the monthly price not include?

Stated plainly, because a content plan with no edges is a content plan that
disappoints someone:

- Professional photography, on-site shoots and doctor filming sessions
- Videographer costs, travel, actors or models
- Advanced animation and high-end motion graphics
- Advertising spend, and Meta Ads management
- Influencer campaigns
- Direct-message and community management, or handling patient enquiries
- Unlimited revisions, or additional platforms beyond the one agreed
- Research-heavy carousels beyond the agreed content capacity

Those are quoted separately, and every rate is
[published on the pricing page](/pricing): complex carousels, extra reel
edits, Meta Ads management and shoots. A carousel is priced on slides,
research and medical complexity rather than at one flat figure, because a
seven-slide researched explainer is not a static post. Your advertising
spend is always paid by you, directly, and never marked up by us.

We do not chase follower counts or buy engagement, and we do not promise
that social media will fill your appointment book. If someone promises you
that, ask them how they will measure it.
