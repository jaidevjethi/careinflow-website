## What a dental patient is doing when they find you

Two people search for a dentist, and they are nothing alike.

The first has a broken tooth or an abscess, it is late, and they are in pain. They are on a phone, on mobile data, and they will look at three results at most. What they need to know is whether you are open, how far away you are, and whether they can reach a human. If your site takes six seconds to load, they are already at the next clinic.

The second has been thinking about aligners or implants for eight months. They are not in a hurry at all. They will read everything you publish, look at your before-and-after photographs, check your reviews, and think about it for another two weeks. What they need is enough detail to feel informed, and enough honesty to feel safe.

A dental website that only serves one of these loses the other.

## The pages that do the work

Dentistry is the specialty where a page per treatment matters most, because the treatments are priced so differently and worry about them so differently. A cleaning is a small decision. An implant is a large one, taken slowly.

Each treatment page answers what that patient came in with: what it costs, whether it hurts, how many visits, how long it lasts, and what happens if they wait. Written plainly, in the words patients use — "tooth pain", not "irreversible pulpitis".

## The part most dental sites get wrong

The emergency path.

Most clinic websites treat urgent care as one line in a list. But an emergency patient is the most valuable visitor a dental practice gets — high intent, immediate, and usually the start of a long relationship — and they are also the least patient. They need your hours, your location and a way to message you within one tap of landing.

We build that path first and test it on a mid-range Android over mobile data, because that is the phone actually in their hand.
